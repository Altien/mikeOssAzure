# About these guides

This section holds short guides to using Mike. Pick one from the contents list
on the left.

Guides explain what a feature does, what it deliberately does not do, and what
to do when something comes back looking wrong. They are written to be read at
the moment you need them rather than in advance.

## What you will and will not find here

Guides describe how the product behaves. They do not give legal advice, and
they do not tell you whether an output is fit for the matter in front of you.
Where a feature has limits — and the ones worth using usually do — the guide
covering it says so plainly. Those sections are the ones to read before relying
on a result.

## Guides your organisation has added

Your firm or legal department can add its own guides here, and they appear in
the same contents list as the ones shipped with the product. If you see
material about your own house style, matter-opening procedure, or who to
contact about a particular kind of work, it was written by your organisation
rather than by Mike.

That is the intended use: one place to look, whether the question is about the
software or about how your organisation expects it to be used.

## If a guide does not answer your question

Guides cover the product's behaviour. They cannot cover your matter, your
client's position, or your organisation's policy. For those, ask the person who
would normally supervise the work — and if a guide left you unsure whether a
result can be relied on, treat that as a reason to check rather than to
proceed.

If something in the product behaves differently from what a guide describes,
tell whoever supports Mike in your organisation. A guide that has drifted from
the software is worth fixing quickly, because the next person to read it will
trust it.
